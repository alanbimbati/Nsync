Nsync

Syncthing, with your devices found through Nostr

How your devices find each other

Nsync is Syncthing with one addition: the way your devices locate each other.

  1. This device writes a short note, signed with a key that exists only for it: “I am device X, reach me at these addresses.”
  2. It publishes the note to the Nostr relays below: public servers that simply store such notes and hand them out.
  3. Your paired devices read the note from the relays and connect to you directly. Your files never pass through the relays.

Why the relays have to know your IP address. For your other devices to connect to this one, they must learn where it is. That address changes (another Wi-Fi, mobile data, your provider), so Nsync looks at it every minute, publishes again as soon as it changes, and at least every 20 minutes; a note stops being valid if this device goes quiet.

What exactly the relays can see

The addresses in the note (your local and public IP and Syncthing's port), and when this device is online, under a key made for this device alone. Not your files, not your folder names. The note is not encrypted: anyone who can read a relay can read it. That is the same thing Syncthing's own discovery servers already learn about your device. If you would rather not share your public IP, switch on Local network only below: then only devices on the same network can find this one this way.
